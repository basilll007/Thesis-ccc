## 1. MISTy
- **What it does.** For each target marker it fits one model per "view": the intraview (other markers in the same cell or spot), a juxtaview (the immediate neighbourhood) and a paraview (a wider radius; in the IMC analysis, cells inside the juxtaview radius are excluded). The paper uses random forests by default and says other explainable ensembles can be substituted. A ridge-regression meta-model combines the view predictions (late fusion). It reports (a) the gain in variance explained of the multiview model over the intraview alone, (b) normalised view contributions and (c) predictor-to-target importances.
- **What it does not do.** It has no chain, mediation or multi-hop term. Each model links one target to predictors in one context. The paper states that the relations "cannot be assumed to be directly causal nor directional", and that most false positives in its in-silico test come "from inferring indirect or higher-order interactions" (Results). In other words, MISTy treats indirect (chain-like) dependencies as a source of false positives rather than as something to test.

## 2. NCEM (node-centric expression models)
- **What it does.** It predicts each cell's expression vector from its own cell type plus its niche (the cells within a chosen radius). Model classes: a linear NCEM with receiver-type x sender-presence interaction terms; a non-linear graph neural network (indicator kernel or one-layer graph convolution); a ligand-receptor graph kernel; and a conditional VAE. Evaluation is held-out R2 against a non-spatial baseline of cell type and domain. The fetched text reports an average gain for linear NCEMs of ΔR2 = 0.016, a peak at an intermediate length scale of about 69 um, and no improvement of the non-linear models over the linear ones. "Cell type coupling" counts significant sender-receiver coefficients.
- **What it does not do.** The neighbourhood is one hop (a radius, or a one-layer graph convolution). There is no chain or mediation analysis, and the dependencies are presented as statistical rather than mechanistic. The paper simulates segmentation errors (counts moved to a neighbouring cell) in its Methods but, in the text read, does not treat spillover as a confounder of the niche effect.

## 3. CellNEST
- **What it does.** A graph attention network over a spatial cell graph scores ligand-receptor edges. By default it keeps the top 20 % of edges by attention. A "relay network" is defined as a ligand-receptor pair from cell i to cell j followed by another from j to k. Relays are found by counting how often such two-hop patterns occur. Their support is a confidence score built from prior-knowledge resources (STRING, NicheNet, DoRothEA). For PDAC it reports frequent two-hop patterns such as FN1-RPSA -> FN1-RPSA and COL1A1-SDC1 -> FN1-RPSA in one main sample, localised mainly to myofibroblast-like CAFs.
- **What it does not do.** In the text read, a relay is a co-occurrence of two high-attention edges. It is not tested as mediation: there is no check that the i->k association shrinks once j is conditioned on. No null that preserves spatial zonation was described for relays. The Discussion states that "the algorithm cannot determine whether the observed ligand expression is truly caused by receptor activation", and the outputs are framed as hypotheses.

## 4. HoloNet (abstract only)
- **What it does.** It builds a multi-view network of cell-cell communication events (one view per ligand-receptor pair) from ligand-receptor pairs, cell-type spatial distribution and downstream gene expression. It trains an attention-based graph model per target gene and interprets the trained model to decode "functional communication events". Applied to three Visium datasets.
- **What it does not do (UNVERIFIED beyond the abstract).** The abstract describes one-hop sender-to-receiver attribution for a target gene. No multi-hop or mediation analysis is mentioned. The full text was not read.

## How this work differs
1. **Explicit two-hop mediation test.** For each receiver cell C, the upstream exposure A is restricted to A cells that touch C's B neighbours but are not themselves adjacent to C. The chain is then tested hop by hop: C ~ B, B ~ A, and C ~ A with and without B. The decision rule requires the A->C association to shrink once B is added, with a tumour-level bootstrap CI on the indirect effect. MISTy and NCEM stop at one hop and treat indirect effects as nuisance. CellNEST counts two-hop co-occurrences but does not test conditional shrinkage.
2. **Zonation-aware nulls and controls.** Every hop has a 200 um tile-level mean of the outcome program as a covariate. Significance comes from block permutations of labels and of expression within 200 um tiles at random offsets, reusing the v3 null module. v3 showed that global, distance-stratified and finer block nulls are fooled by zonation (docs/PDAC_SUMMARY_v3.md, Phase 2).
3. **Calibration by simulation before interpretation.** Chains, zonation-only fields and direct A->C effects are planted into real sections at known fold changes. This gives power and false-positive rates of the exact decision rule, and Phase 3 is interpreted only inside the calibrated range.
4. **Tumour as the unit of inference, plus negative controls.** The negative controls are the reversed chain, a random substitute for B and shuffled B scores. The reversed chain tests whether direction is identifiable at all from undirected spatial adjacency, a point none of the four papers tests directly.
5. **Spillover as an explicit confounder.** Segmentation spillover can make a cell look like its neighbours. NCEM simulates it but, in the text read, does not adjust for it. Here the receiver's own score on the sender's lineage markers is a pre-declared sensitivity covariate.
6. **Output is ranked hypotheses for perturbation, not discovered mechanisms.** Ligand-receptor names come from the existing catalogue and are labelled "candidate". Expression is not mutation. Associations are not causal.
